## Before the session

Bring your classified timeline from Week 1. It ends at a meeting: the
2027-03-08 Steering Committee that confirmed the ClarityPulse rollout
timeline. This week's document is that meeting's invitation list.

An invitation and a decision right are not the same thing. Being in the room
is not the same as having a vote, and having a vote is not the same as being
the person the decision is actually done to. The list below states, for each
person, whether they were invited, what kind of decision right the invitation
carried, and whatever reason the record gives — which is often none at all.

### Case file: the 2027-03-08 Steering Committee invite list

| Name | Title | Invited? | Decision right | Reason given |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Priya Nandakumar | Deputy Vice-Chancellor (Operations) | Yes | Vote | Chairs the committee |
| Tomas Herrera | Director of Digital Delivery | Yes | Vote | Owns the implementation |
| Beatrix Oyelaran | Head of Finance & Risk | Yes | Vote | Budget holder |
| Sanjay Okoro | Staff Association Rep (Professional Staff) | Yes | Advise | Represents affected professional staff |

This is the same teaching fiction as Week 1 — a case built for this course,
not a real institution's records.

## In the session

For **Dr Femi Adisa** and **LSS frontline staff**, each pair produces:

1. One plausible **legitimate** reason for the exclusion (something about the
   committee's actual terms of reference, not a guess about motive).
2. One plausible **cost** the exclusion has for the decision's quality or
   fairness.
3. One thing — a document, a policy, a fact you don't currently have — that
   would help you tell which reading is closer to right.

### What good analysis of one row looks like

**Dr Femi Adisa, University Librarian — not invited, informed after, no
reason given.**

A legitimate reading: the committee's terms of reference name only
directorate-level roles as members. That is a structural rule applied
consistently, not a decision made about this pilot specifically — the same
rule would exclude any unit head, on any topic, in any week. Read this way,
the exclusion is a side effect of how the committee is constituted, not
evidence of anything about ClarityPulse.

A costly reading: whatever the rule's origin, its effect here is that the
person leading the unit the pilot is rolled out to first has no seat at the
meeting that confirms the timeline for that rollout. A structural rule and a
one-sided effect are not mutually exclusive — both readings can be true at
once, and the interesting question is not which one is "the real reason" but
whether the terms of reference should still exclude this seat given what this
particular decision affects.

What would help decide more: whether the terms of reference have ever been
amended to add a seat for a specifically affected unit on a previous decision.
If yes, the "just a structural rule" reading weakens. If no such amendment has
ever happened for any pilot, it strengthens.

## A little further reading

Sherry Arnstein, ["A Ladder of Citizen
Participation"](https://doi.org/10.1080/01944366908977225), *Journal of the
American Institute of Planners* 35(4), 1969 — a genuinely foundational,
findable paper, not written for this course. Read the description of the
middle rungs
("informing," "consultation," "placation") against the top rungs
("partnership," "delegated power," "citizen control"). Bring one answer:
where would you place "invited, informed after" on that ladder, and is it
above or below "invited to advise, no vote"?

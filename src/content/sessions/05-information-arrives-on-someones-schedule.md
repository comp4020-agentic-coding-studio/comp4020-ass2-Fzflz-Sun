## Before the session

Think of a time you received information "just in time" to act on it — a
schedule change, a budget update, a policy revision. Note who released it,
and how much time you actually had to respond once you had it.

## The case document for this week

**Memo, drafted 17 March, sent 21 March, Beatrix Oyelaran (Finance and Risk
Lead) to the LSS Rollout Working Group — one day before the Working Group's
next scheduled meeting, at which budget sign-off for Phase 2 was expected:**

> **Risk Register Addendum: Engagement Signal Retention**
>
> Flagging for the record ahead of tomorrow's meeting: ClarityPulse's default
> configuration retains individual-level engagement scores for 24 months,
> not the 6 months referenced in the original proposal. Confirming this
> required a written answer from the vendor, received today. Recommend the
> Working Group consider a contractual retention cap before Phase 2 spend is
> approved.

## In the session

Whoever controls when a document lands controls, in part, how much
scrutiny it gets — not because the content changes, but because reading
speed doesn't scale with meeting length. This week separates two things
that are easy to collapse into one: releasing information late, and
releasing information in a way designed to foreclose response. The first
is often just how organisations actually work — a report waits on a final
sign-off, a figure isn't confirmed until the day before. The second uses
that same ordinary-looking lateness as cover.

Working from the memo above, the session asks what would distinguish the
two readings in a paper trail this thin: whether the same pattern of
lateness recurs regardless of who benefits, whether an earlier draft
existed and was held back, and whether the recipients had any standing way
to ask for more time and didn't take it. The memo itself does not settle
this — it states only that the confirming answer "was received today," and
whether that is the honest shape of when Finance actually learned the fact,
or a convenient thing to say, is exactly the gap you cannot close from this
document alone.

## Before the session

Your [Alignment Lab](/assessments/alignment-lab/) submission — strategy
memo, negotiation log, decision record, individual autopsy and partial
rerun — is due today at noon, before this session runs, in full: the
scored partial rerun is not something you do live in today's session, it
is part of what you hand in beforehand. Submit it first, then reread your
group's decision record as if you were a stranger to the process, using
only what it actually says.

## In the session

This week asks a question the Lab's process rules deliberately don't
answer for you: is a decision process fair because everyone had a formal
turn to speak, or does fairness require something more — equal capacity to
change the outcome, not just equal airtime? The [Alignment Lab](/alignment-lab/)
is explicit that it never grades on negotiation skill or role-goal
achievement; this session is where the class discusses what, if anything,
that design choice owes the role that structurally has the least leverage.

As a supplementary, ungraded exercise — distinct from the scored partial
rerun you already submitted this morning — groups replay one contact
decision differently as a group and discuss what, if anything, changes
about the outcome or the record, the same move demonstrated in the Lab's
own worked example. This is a live discussion, not a second rerun to hand
in; it exists so you can compare notes with people who ran a different
change than the one in your own written rerun.

## After the session

Nothing further is due immediately. Hold onto today's discussion,
alongside the ethical reading this session practised — you will draw on
both when the [Decision System Redesign](/assessments/decision-system-redesign/)
asks you to name what your own process cost the people with the least
leverage in it.

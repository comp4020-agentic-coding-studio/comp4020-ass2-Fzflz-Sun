## Before the session

Recall a time you disagreed with a group decision but the final record made
no mention of it. What would you have needed to write, at the time, for
that disagreement to be traceable afterwards?

## The case documents for this week

Weeks 6 and 7 gave you two records of the same six minutes of the 13 March
LSS Rollout Working Group meeting — Week 6's an excerpt, not the complete
minutes document. Placed side by side, the gap between what each shows is
exact and countable:

| In the transcript (Week 7) | In the excerpted minutes (Week 6) |
|---|---|
| Okoro asks whether frontline staff were consulted on the order, and questions why Circulation goes first | "Sanjay Okoro asked whether frontline staff would have a chance to raise concerns" — the specific challenge is not recorded, only that a question occurred |
| Castellano's comms suggestion is folded in, though never by name | Not mentioned in the excerpt |
| Nandakumar moves on without answering Okoro | "The sequence was confirmed as presented" — reads as a neutral outcome |

**Dissent scenarios for the exercise below** — two further, shorter
moments from the same pilot, for you to classify and rewrite:

> **Scenario A.** Minutes record: "Dr Adisa raised concerns about staff
> capacity during the pilot period. Noted." No response, no follow-up
> action, and no indication whether "noted" meant anything changed.
>
> **Scenario B.** Minutes record: "Okoro objected that the professional
> staff seat does not constitute LSS-specific consultation, and asked that
> this be revisited before Phase 2. Action: Herrera to respond in writing
> within two weeks." The objection, its grounds, and a concrete follow-up
> are all present.

## In the session

"Discussion was held" is the single most common way a real disagreement
disappears from a record — not through suppression, usually, but through
the ordinary pressure to keep minutes short and move on. This session
practices writing a dissent that survives: specific about what is disputed,
specific about the grounds, and specific about whether it is a full
objection, a conditional objection, or a request to revisit later.

Using the [decision record template](/templates/), the class first
diagnoses what Scenario A above is missing that Scenario B has, then
rewrites Scenario A's minuted line so that a reader six months later could
reconstruct what was actually disputed and why — checking each rewrite
against the transcript-versus-minutes gap in the table above.

## After the session

This is the format the Alignment Lab's own decision record will need from
Week 9 onward — dissent that is conditional or partial support is treated
there as a legitimate outcome, not a failure to reach consensus.

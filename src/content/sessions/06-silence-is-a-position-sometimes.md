## Before the session

This is the week the [Decision Autopsy](/assessments/decision-autopsy/)
assessment is due. Bring your finished autopsy, including whatever gaps you
found and could not close.

## The case document for this week

**Minutes excerpt, LSS Rollout Working Group, 13 March, circulated 20
March:**

> **Item 2: Phased rollout sequence.** Tomas Herrera presented the proposed
> three-phase sequence. Sanjay Okoro asked whether frontline staff would
> have a chance to raise concerns about phase order before it was finalised.
> **The sequence was confirmed as presented.** Item closed, 6 minutes.

## In the session

An abstention, a name missing from a distribution list, or a minute that
records "discussion held" with no summary are all silences — and all of
them are compatible with several different explanations. Someone abstains
because they are conflicted, because they disagree but don't want it
minuted, because they weren't paying attention, or because abstaining is
the position their role requires regardless of their private view. The
record alone cannot tell you which.

Read the minutes excerpt above: Okoro's question is recorded; nothing
between it and "confirmed as presented" is. Working in pairs, list every
distinct explanation for that gap that the minutes alone cannot rule out —
before the class compares lists and checks whether anyone's Decision
Autopsy this week resolved a similar gap by assumption rather than by
evidence.

The session then runs a short peer exercise: swap autopsies with a partner
and try to find a silence in their chosen decision that admits a reading
they didn't consider. The goal is not to win an argument about what
"really" happened — it is to widen the set of explanations a reader is
required to consider before treating a silence as evidence of anything in
particular.

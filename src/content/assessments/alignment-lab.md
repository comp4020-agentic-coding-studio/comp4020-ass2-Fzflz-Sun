## The brief

> Play one of four roles in a decision you do not control the outcome of, then
> go back alone and work out what actually happened.

The full process — the four roles, the pre-meeting contact window, the live
meeting, and the individual audit that follows it — is described in full on
[The Alignment Lab](/alignment-lab/) page, including the fairness and safety
rules that apply before anyone is assigned a role. Read that page before this
one; this page marks the work, that page runs it.

## What you submit

- **Initial judgment** (individual, before role cards are distributed): a
  short, dated statement of what you think should happen, based only on the
  public materials.
- **Strategy memo** (individual, before contact opens): my responsibilities,
  my evidence, what I don't know, who I plan to contact, and my ethical
  boundaries — the same five fields as the [strategy memo
  template](/templates/).
- **Negotiation log** (individual): a record of every contact you made or
  received — its purpose, any new information, any commitments, and whether
  your judgment changed — or a note that you chose not to make contact, and
  why.
- **Decision record** (one per group): the outcome, in the format on the
  [Templates](/templates/) page.
- **Decision autopsy** (individual, after the reveal): 500–700 words reaching
  one of three conclusions about what actually decided the outcome, versus
  what the decision record says decided it — a specific distortion the
  record and log support, no significant distortion evident, or
  indeterminate on what you have (naming the specific evidence that's
  missing, the alternative explanation you could not rule out without it,
  and what would change your judgment if it turned up). Indeterminate is not
  a shrug: it is argued to the same standard of evidence as the other two.
- **Partial rerun** (individual): a short re-run of one specific juncture
  from the negotiation or meeting, testing whether one named process change
  would have produced a different outcome.

## How it is marked

Everything above is marked per person, not per group, with one exception:
two people in the same room can submit very different strategy memos, logs
and audits, and are marked on their own work throughout. The decision record
is the one artefact a live group produces together, and is marked once and
applied to everyone named as a participant in it. None of the five criteria
mark negotiation win/loss, performance skill, how often you spoke, or whether
your role's stated goal was achieved — they mark how you used evidence,
adapted to what you learned, and reasoned about the outcome afterwards.

Missing the live meeting, or missing role assignment and the contact window
entirely, does not change these five criteria or their weights — it changes
which pathway supplies the evidence for them. The [Alignment
Lab](/alignment-lab/) page's "What you submit" section sets out, criterion by
criterion, exactly what the live, async make-up, and alternative /
structured-async pathways each submit, and states explicitly why each is held
to the same evidentiary standard rather than a lighter one.

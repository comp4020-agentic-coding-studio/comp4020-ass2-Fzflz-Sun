## The brief

> Take a decision-making process — the semester's case, your Alignment Lab
> group, or a real process you have access to — and redesign it so that the
> next decision it produces is harder to make in the meeting before the
> meeting.

This is the course's thesis turned into a design problem. Weeks 1–10 taught
you to read a process for where its real decisions happen; the Alignment Lab
gave you first-hand evidence of how your own group's process actually worked,
against how it looked on paper. This assessment asks you to use that evidence
to design something better, not just to diagnose what was wrong.

## What you submit

A redesign document, 1,800–2,400 words, that:

1. Names the process being redesigned and who currently makes its decisions.
2. States what is wrong with it today, citing specific evidence — from the
   semester's case, from your own Alignment Lab audit, or both.
3. Proposes a concrete replacement: who is invited, when, on what information,
   with what record kept and by whom.
4. Names who loses influence under the new design, and does not pretend the
   redesign is free.
5. Tests the replacement against at least one concrete scenario — a specific
   decision the current process would have to face — and walks through what
   the new design would actually produce, step by step.

A redesign that makes everyone equally powerful is not a redesign; it is a
wish. The strongest submissions make a specific, defensible trade-off and
argue for it.

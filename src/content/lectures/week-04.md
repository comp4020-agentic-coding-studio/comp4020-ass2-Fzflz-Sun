## One document is rarely enough

Week 3 asked you to read a single agenda for what it argued. This week's
question is different: what does it take for *several* documents, none of
them individually damning, to add up to something a single document never
could? The honest answer has a ceiling built into it. A pattern across
documents raises the burden the "genuinely open" reading has to meet — it
does not retire that reading. Treating a stack of circumstantial signals as
equivalent to a confession is the single most common overreach this course
marks down, on either side of the argument.

## The story continues: a working session before the working group

The Steering Committee confirmed the ClarityPulse implementation timeline on
8 March. What happened in the week after is new material — two documents
that were not part of Week 1's fact/inference exercise, because they did not
exist yet: a calendar invite, sent 10 March, for a small working session
between Tomas Herrera, Priya Nandakumar and ClarityPulse's delivery team,
and the agenda for the 13 March LSS Rollout Working Group the invite refers
to as "Saturday." Both documents are reproduced in full on the [session
page](/sessions/04-the-meeting-before-the-meeting/) — read them there before
continuing, since the reading below assumes you have.

## What stacks here, and what doesn't

Read alone, the calendar invite is unremarkable — technical delivery
planning ahead of a large meeting is a completely normal reason for a small
group to talk first, and the course does not ask you to treat every
pre-meeting conversation as suspicious. Read alone, five minutes for an
agenda item with an attachment already prepared is also unremarkable — plenty
of agenda items are genuinely simple. What starts to stack is the
combination: the small group's stated purpose is explicitly "so we're not
designing it live," the attached sequence in the 13 March agenda is not
described as a proposal open to reordering, and no version of the sequence
that differs from the working session's outcome appears anywhere in the
record. None of that proves the 40-person Working Group's discussion was
theatre. It does mean that if you wanted to argue it wasn't, you now have to
point to something in the room on 13 March that the documents alone don't
show — which is exactly the gap the honest answer has to name.

## The honest counter-case, stated plainly

A technical sequence — which team goes first — is a reasonable thing for a
delivery lead and a sponsor to work out before spending a large meeting's
time on it, provided the *sequence* was the only thing pre-narrowed and the
Working Group still had a real chance to weigh in on the parts the small
group didn't already decide. Whether that's what actually happened here is
not settled by these two documents alone; Week 6's Decision Autopsy is where
you'll be asked to make and defend a call like this on a real decision of
your own choosing.

## Placing the two records side by side

Week 6 gave you an excerpt of the minutes covering Item 2 from the 13
March Working Group. Week 7 gave you a transcript of the same six minutes.
Placed side by side, the gap between what each shows is exact and
countable — with one caveat: "not mentioned" below describes the excerpt
you were given, not a claim about the complete minutes document, which no
material in this course reproduces in full.

| In the transcript | In the excerpted minutes |
|---|---|
| Okoro asks whether frontline staff were consulted on the order, and questions why Circulation goes first | "Sanjay Okoro asked whether frontline staff would have a chance to raise concerns" — the *content* of the question (the order itself, and why Circulation) is not recorded, only that a question of some kind occurred |
| Castellano's comms suggestion is folded straight into the plan, though the chair never actually names her while doing it | Not mentioned in the excerpt — an accepted suggestion left no trace in what you were shown, either |
| Nandakumar moves on without answering Okoro | "The sequence was confirmed as presented" — reads as a neutral outcome, not as a chair moving past an unanswered question |

Two things disappear in the compression from transcript to this excerpt,
and they disappear differently. Okoro's specific challenge is smoothed
into a generic "asked whether... would have a chance" — technically not
false, but stripped of the part that made it a substantive objection
rather than a procedural one. Castellano's adopted suggestion is simply
absent from what you can see of the minutes — consistent with "genuinely
left out of the full document" and with "recorded somewhere this excerpt
doesn't cover," and the record itself can't tell you which; an accepted
contribution needs no defending the way a rejected one might, which is
one reason it could be dropped either way. Minuting is not neutral
compression; it compresses unevenly, and which parts survive tells you
almost as much as what was actually said — provided you stay honest about
how much of the full document you're actually looking at.

## What this means for writing your own dissent

If a specific, substantive objection can be minuted into a generic
"raised a question" without anyone technically lying, the practical lesson
is that a dissent's *survival* in the record is not guaranteed by having
been spoken — it has to be made hard to compress. Three moves help, none of
them requiring a confrontational tone:

- **Ask for it in writing, in your own words, attached to the minutes** —
  "could my objection be recorded as: 'Okoro objected that Circulation's
  high public-facing hours make it a poor first-phase choice, and asked for
  frontline consultation before the order is finalised'" is a request a
  chair can grant in seconds, and it survives compression because it
  arrives already compressed to the version you chose.
- **Ask for a response to be minuted, not just the question** — "confirmed
  as presented" reads as neutral only because no answer sits next to the
  question in the text. Asking, in the room, "can the minutes note that
  this wasn't addressed before the item closed" turns an absence into a
  fact on the record.
- **Follow up in writing afterwards if the minutes still smooth it over** —
  a dated email restating the objection, sent after minutes circulate,
  creates a second document that a compressed minute cannot erase.

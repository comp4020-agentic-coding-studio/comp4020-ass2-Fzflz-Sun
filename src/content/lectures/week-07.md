## The same six minutes, a third time

Week 4 gave you the agenda for the 13 March meeting. Week 6 gave you its
minutes. Neither shows what actually happened between the question and the
confirmation. This week gives you a transcript excerpt of exactly that gap
— not because a transcript is somehow more truthful than an agenda or
minutes, but because it shows a different kind of evidence: not what was
planned, not what was recorded as the outcome, but who spoke, in what
order, and how much friction each contribution met before it counted.

## Two suggestions, two very different receptions

Two people speak after Herrera's sequence is presented. Sanjay Okoro raises
a substantive question about *whether the order is right* — a question
about the decision itself. Wren Castellano offers something about *how the
decision will be communicated* — a question downstream of the decision
already being made. The chair's "good" lands immediately after Castellano's
comms offer, before he turns to the sequence — the natural reading is that
he is taking her suggestion up, since nothing about it gets questioned or
deferred before he moves to closing the item. That reading is an inference,
not a transcribed fact: "good" is one syllable, and a real alternative
explanation exists — a chair working through a fixed agenda might say
"good" as pure momentum-filler without registering Castellano's comment as
a distinct thing to accept or reject at all, in which case nothing was
actually adopted, only left unexamined the same way Okoro's question was.
The transcript that would settle it — Circulation actually being briefed
the way she offered — is not part of what you were given. What the
transcript can support without that extra inference is narrower but still
real: her comment was not challenged, and Okoro's was not addressed at all.
The chair responds to Okoro's question by not responding to it at all,
moving directly to closing the item. Neither speaker was interrupted,
talked over, or told to be quiet — this is not a transcript of anyone
behaving badly. It is a transcript of two contributions being sorted, in
real time, by whether they questioned the outcome or helped implement it,
with only one kind getting any acknowledgement at all.

This is what "power in the room" means for this course: not who is loudest,
but whose *kind* of contribution the chair treats as actionable. A chair
does not need to silence dissent for a room to reliably favour agreement —
routinely responding to the comment that helps and passing over the comment
that complicates produces the same effect, meeting after meeting, without a
single person ever being told to stop talking.

## The honest counter-case, stated plainly

A chair managing four more agenda items in a fixed slot has a real,
legitimate reason to move a meeting along, and "let's keep momentum" is an
ordinary thing to say in that position — it does not require bad faith to
explain. What the transcript cannot tell you, and what you should resist
guessing at, is whether Okoro's question would have been picked up given
more time, or whether "momentum" was the excuse rather than the reason.
That is exactly the kind of call the Decision Autopsy asks you to make and
defend — including a rigorously argued case that the record is genuinely
indeterminate on this point, if that is honestly where the evidence leads.
What it does not accept is the shortcut version of the same word: calling
the record "inconclusive" without naming what evidence is missing, what
specific alternative you cannot rule out, and what would resolve it — that
is avoiding the work, not doing it.

## A little further reading

The linked page is a secondary overview of French and Raven's five bases of
social power (coercive, reward, legitimate, referent, expert) — useful here
as a checklist, not as a citation for this week's own claims about the 13
March transcript, which rest on the transcript itself, not on this
framework. Read it for one purpose: name which one or two of the five bases
best explain why the chair's "good" landed so easily after Castellano's
comment, and which explain why Okoro's got nothing — bring both to Week 8.

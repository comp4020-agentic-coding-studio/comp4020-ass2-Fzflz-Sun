# Three Kinds of Claim

Week 1 — before you can read a decision, you need to sort what a document proves

---

## The whole course is this move

Reading an agenda, an invitation list, a silence, a set of minutes — every
later week is this same distinction, applied to a different document.

- **Fact** — a dated document states it directly
- **Inference** — a reasonable reading, one step removed
- **Unknown** — the record does not settle it either way

---

## Not the same move

"The document is dated 3 March" is a fact.

"The document being dated 3 March proves the decision was already made" is an
inference — a different sentence, doing more work than the first one.

Confusing the two is the single most common mistake this course marks against.

---

## Unknown is not a failure

Saying "the public record does not show this" is itself a finding.

It is not the same as saying "I could not be bothered to find out."

The Engagement Signal Pilot, January–March. Same case, all semester.

## The seam

Between 1 March (open framing) and 3 March (settled framing), something
changed.

**Inference, not fact:** no document names who, or why.

**An equally reasonable alternative:** the question may have been genuinely
settled earlier, off the record, for good reasons — not concealed.

---

## What this course is not claiming

Not: every meeting is theatre, and every private conversation before it is
manipulation.

Pre-meeting coordination can make a room better informed. It can also exclude
people who should have had a say. The public record often cannot tell you
which happened — only what it can and cannot show.

---

{/* _class: quote */}

> A primary source records what a particular document or participant states
> happened. It does not itself guarantee the record is complete or true, it
> rarely tells you why, and it never tells you who else was in the room but
> not on the page.

A working description this course holds itself to, not a citation

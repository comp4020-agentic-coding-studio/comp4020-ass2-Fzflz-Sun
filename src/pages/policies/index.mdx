# Policies and support

These are Slop University's course-wide rules for SLOP4700, not a real
institution's policy — they exist so each assessment brief can link to one
place instead of restating the same rules three times.

## Collaboration and citation

You may discuss the Engagement Signal Pilot case, the readings and general
approaches with classmates. What you submit — your strategy memo, your
negotiation log, your Decision Autopsy, your redesign proposal — must be your
own analysis in your own words. If a classmate's observation genuinely shaped
your thinking, say so in a line of acknowledgement; it costs you nothing and
protects you from an integrity question you didn't need to have. Cite every
reading you rely on, including the case documents themselves when you quote
their wording directly. An unattributed idea that turns out to belong to
someone else is treated as a citation failure first and escalated only if the
pattern repeats.

## The Alignment Lab is not an exception

Everything above applies to the Lab too, with one addition: the formal
decision meeting itself is a live, unscripted event, not a script you can
prepare in advance with a partner from your role group. Comparing notes on
the case facts before role assignment is fine. Coordinating in advance what
you will each say in the meeting, or sharing your strategy memo with
someone playing another role, defeats the point of the exercise and is
treated as a collaboration breach on that assessment.

This does not restrict the Lab's own required pre-meeting contact window —
making a self-initiated contact within the limit described on the
[Alignment Lab](/alignment-lab/) page, exchanging evidence in that contact,
or recording a commitment made during it, is the assessment's own mechanic
and is expected, not a breach. What the rule above prohibits is scripting
in advance what either side will say, ghost-writing another role's lines
or log entries, or bypassing the contact limit — not the exchange the
window is there for.

## Absence, deferral and extensions

Tell your tutor before a session you know you will miss, where that's
possible. The Alignment Lab's role assignment and pre-meeting contact
window both open once, in Week 9's session — the decision meeting itself
happens afterwards, self-arranged by your group within the 48–72 hour
window that follows, not inside the Week 9 class. Missing Week 9's session
still means missing your role assignment and the start of your window, which
is exactly the case the [Alignment Lab](/alignment-lab/) page routes to its
alternative / structured-async pathway rather than the ordinary async
make-up, so if you know in advance you cannot attend that session, contact
your tutor at least a week ahead so that pathway can be set up for you rather
than improvised on the day. For the two written assessments, a short extension for illness or
misadventure is available on request, made before the due date where
possible; unrequested late work is accepted but marked down per day late
unless an extension was agreed first.

## Getting help

Ask during a session first — most questions about the case, the templates or
what a criterion is actually asking for are faster to resolve out loud than
by email, and the answer usually helps three other people who had the same
question. Outside sessions, email the person whose [page](/people/) lists the
relevant responsibility, and allow two working days for a reply. If a
response from your tutor doesn't resolve the issue, the convenor is the
escalation point — say in your email that you've already asked your tutor and
what they said, so the convenor isn't starting from nothing.

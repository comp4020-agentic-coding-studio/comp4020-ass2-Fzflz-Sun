The Alignment Lab is the course's central exercise: a small group is given a
real-shaped decision to make together, each person holding one of four roles
and a private brief the others cannot see in full. It is marked as
[The Alignment Lab](/assessments/alignment-lab/) assessment, worth 45% of the
course.

The scenario used for the graded Lab is confidential and distributed by the
teaching team before your group's session — publishing it here would let
later groups prepare against roles they haven't been assigned yet, which
defeats the exercise. This page instead runs you through the process using a
small, fully public worked example, so you can see exactly what a role card
and a decision record look like before you are handed the real ones.

## The four roles

Every Lab scenario is built around the same four functional archetypes, even
though the fictional person and organisation change each time:

- **Executive Sponsor** — owns the outcome the organisation wants, and
  answers for it upward.
- **Delivery Lead** — has to make whatever gets decided actually work.
- **Finance and Risk Lead** — controls whether the decision can be funded,
  and what it might cost if it goes wrong.
- **Staff Representative** — speaks for the people the decision is done to,
  not just the people making it.

Each role's private brief carries the same six things: a public
responsibility everyone can see, one piece of evidence only that role holds,
one constraint they cannot trade away, one form of leverage or dependency
over the others, the minimum outcome they can live with, and the specific
condition under which they would change their position.

## Ground rules, before anyone is assigned a role

These apply to every run of the Lab, and are agreed before role cards are
distributed — not introduced partway through, and not something you discover
only in Week 11's material on the ethics of alignment.

- **No real disputes.** Every scenario is fictional. You will never be asked
  to re-litigate an actual disagreement from your workplace, household or
  student group.
- **Opt-out without penalty.** If a role or scenario would put you in a
  position you are not comfortable with, you can request a swap or an
  alternative individual task before the session, no explanation required.
- **In-role, not in-character.** You are arguing a position, not performing a
  persona. Personal attacks, real names, and anything outside the brief are
  out of bounds, and the session facilitator can pause the room if the line
  is crossed.
- **Nothing said in-role is evidence of what you personally believe.** The
  decision autopsy afterwards is marked on your analysis, not on the position
  your role required you to argue.
- **The record is the record.** Whatever the group's decision record says is
  what gets marked as the group's outcome, even if you privately disagreed —
  which is precisely the situation the decision autopsy asks you to examine.
- **Absence doesn't erase information.** Every role carries a short
  continuity memo alongside its brief, kept up to date through the
  pre-meeting window, so that if the person holding a role is unexpectedly
  absent, the group still has what they knew.
- **Make-up and async participation are always available.** If you miss the
  live meeting, you complete an equivalent asynchronous version rather than
  losing the mark — and no one's grade depends on a groupmate showing up.
- **Peer feedback checks contributions, not popularity.** Any peer input into
  marking asks what a specific person actually contributed — a piece of
  evidence surfaced, a commitment logged, a position changed — never a
  general vote on who did well.
- **You exit the role afterwards.** Once the reveal has happened, the group
  explicitly closes the exercise before moving on: what a role did in the
  simulation is discussed as the role's behaviour, not as a judgement of the
  classmate who played it.

## How a run of the Lab works

0. **Rehearsal.** A small, ungraded run of this whole process — using the
   public worked example below — before anyone sits the assessed Lab.
1. **Public materials and initial judgment.** Before any role is assigned,
   everyone reads the same public materials and submits an individual
   initial judgment: what they think should happen, on the record, before
   anyone has a stake in a role.
2. **Role assignment and strategy memo.** Each person receives one
   confidential role brief and, individually, submits a one-page strategy
   memo: their responsibilities, their evidence, what they don't know, who
   they plan to contact, and the ethical boundaries they won't cross.
3. **Pre-meeting contact window** (48–72 hours). By default, each person may
   make at most **two self-initiated contacts** — but can reply to as many
   requests from others as they like. Every contact, sent or received, is
   logged by both parties: its purpose, any new information, any commitments
   made, and whether either person's judgment changed. The simulator below
   shows the shape of this window using the public worked example.
4. **The meeting.** Live or synchronous, one sitting of around 25 minutes, no
   extensions on the clock once it starts.
5. **Decision record.** The group produces one record — the decision or a
   reasoned deferral, who holds final authority over it, evidence versus
   unverified assumption, unresolved dissent, who owns the resulting actions,
   and what would reopen it — using the
   [decision record template](/templates/). Consensus is not required.
6. **Reveal and consequence test.** Every role brief is shared with the whole
   group, then a choice-based consequence test runs against the decision
   record. Its outcomes come from explicit, traceable case rules, not from
   chance or dramatic licence — it is a teaching model of one plausible
   consequence, not a claim that a real decision like it would play out the
   same way.
7. **Individual autopsy and partial rerun.** Individually: a decision
   autopsy naming what you now think actually decided the outcome, then a
   short rerun of one specific juncture to test whether a named process
   change would have produced a different one.

## Worked example: should the Reading Room move to hot-desking?

This is a small, entirely public scenario used only to demonstrate the
mechanics above — it is not the graded Lab scenario, and none of the
documents below are marked on how convincing they sound, only on the
evidence and reasoning inside them. It walks through every artefact a real
run produces, end to end, with an example filled in at each step.

Facilities wants to convert 40 assigned desks in the Reading Room into
unassigned hot-desks, to fit a larger cohort. Staff were not consulted before
the proposal was drafted. Four people, one for each archetype, have to reach
a decision.

### 1. Public materials and an example initial judgment

Everyone reads the paragraph above — the same materials, before any role is
assigned — and records what they think should happen. Here is one example of
a filled-in initial judgment:

> Converting to hot-desking might be defensible if utilisation really is
> low, but a proposal drafted with no staff input is a bad way to get there
> even if the destination is right. Before I'd support it, I would want to
> see the actual utilisation data behind the proposal, and confirmation that
> anyone with a documented accessibility need has been asked what they
> require. My initial position: support investigating the change, do not
> support committing to a date yet.

This is submitted on the record before role assignment specifically so it
can later be compared with what the same person argued once they were
holding a role — the gap between the two is often the most interesting
thing in the decision autopsy.

### 3. A filled example strategy memo

Individually, each person turns their role card into a one-page memo before
contacting anyone. Here is the Staff Representative's:

> **My responsibilities:** speak for the 12 staff who currently have an
> assigned desk, and specifically flag anything that affects the 3 with
> fitted accessibility equipment.
>
> **My evidence:** three staff have accessibility equipment permanently
> fitted to their current desk that a hot-desk cannot accommodate without
> notice. I don't yet have a cost estimate for reserved-desk hardware, or
> know whether Facilities has already budgeted for it.
>
> **What I don't know:** whether the booking software has any bearing on
> cost at all, whether the fit-out has been costed by anyone yet, and
> whether Finance would treat these as one proposal or two.
>
> **Who I plan to contact:** the Delivery Lead first, since they own
> whatever the room's technical side involves, to ask what it would
> actually take to guarantee reserved desks and whether that raises a cost
> I don't already know about; the Finance and Risk Lead only if that
> conversation raises a number I need confirmed.
>
> **Ethical boundaries I won't cross:** I will not agree to any timeline
> that has not been taken back to affected staff for comment, regardless of
> what it would cost the group to wait.

### 4. An example negotiation log

The log is not capped at two entries — it is the two *self-initiated*
contacts that are capped; replies to requests from others, and any
follow-up on the same contact, are logged too. Purpose, new information,
any commitment made, and whether either side's judgment changed are all
recorded for every entry:

1. **Staff Representative → Delivery Lead** (self-initiated). *Purpose:*
   find out if the software upgrade and accessibility fit-out could be one
   proposal. *New information:* drawing on the licence terms only they had
   read (their stated leverage on the role card), the Delivery Lead confirms
   the booking software already supports named, reserved desks at no extra
   cost — only genuine unassigned hot-desking needs the paid upgrade.
   *Commitment:* Delivery Lead agrees to draft a combined line item.
   *Judgment change:* Staff Representative's minimum acceptable outcome now
   looks achievable without a fight — a material change from the initial
   judgment above.
2. **Finance and Risk Lead → Staff Representative** (reply to a request).
   *Purpose:* Finance asked what the accessibility fit-out would cost, to
   fold it into the same costed proposal as the software upgrade.
   *New information:* Staff Representative did not have a number, but named
   the Facilities staff member who does. *Commitment:* Staff Representative
   will chase that number before the meeting. *Judgment change:* none
   recorded either side.
3. **Staff Representative → Executive Sponsor** (self-initiated, second and
   final self-initiated contact). *Purpose:* check whether a slower
   phase-in would still count toward this year's target. *New information:*
   Executive Sponsor confirms they would accept a slower phase-in if it
   comes with a firm date. *Commitment:* none made either way. *Judgment
   change:* Executive Sponsor's position moves from "any plan, fast" toward
   "a firm date matters more than speed" — a change worth bringing into the
   meeting.

### 5. A formal decision record example

The record the group actually produces does not have to be a clean
agreement — dissent, conditional support and deferral are all legitimate
outcomes, provided the record says so honestly:

> **Decision:** proceed with a combined proposal — booking-software upgrade
> and reserved accessibility desks — as one costed line item, phased in
> over two terms with reserved desks guaranteed from day one.
>
> **Evidence relied on:** the booking-software licence terms (Delivery
> Lead); the accessibility equipment list (Staff Representative); the
> utilisation report (Executive Sponsor).
>
> **Unverified assumption:** that the accessibility fit-out cost, still
> unconfirmed at meeting time, will not exceed the software upgrade saving
> Finance identified.
>
> **Dissent:** Finance and Risk Lead supports the plan **conditionally** —
> on record as not approving final spend until the fit-out figure is
> confirmed in writing.
>
> **Action owner:** Delivery Lead drafts the combined proposal; Staff
> Representative confirms the fit-out figure within one week.
>
> **Final decision authority:** Executive Sponsor signs off the *proposal
> going forward* once the fit-out figure lands — not the group by majority.
> That sign-off authorises drafting and costing the combined line item; it
> does not itself authorise spending. Any actual spend still requires the
> Finance and Risk Lead's separate sign-off under their standing authority
> over spend above the university's threshold, which this record does not
> and cannot override.
>
> **What would reopen this record:** a fit-out figure that exceeds the
> software-upgrade saving, or Facilities confirming the "no extra cost"
> licence claim was wrong.

### 6. Consequences, from stated rules

The consequence test is not a dramatised outcome — it applies explicit,
ordered rules to whatever the decision record actually says, so the same
record always produces the same consequence. The rules are checked in this
order, because a record can honestly satisfy more than one trigger at
once — "no named authority" and "defers spend pending a missing figure" are
independent facts about a record, not opposite ends of one switch, so a
record missing both can trip both at the same time unless something decides
which one governs:

1. **Is there a named final authority?** If no → the Space Committee sends
   the record back for one revision before it will consider anything else
   about it. This is checked first: without someone named to hold the
   record's spend decisions, there is no one for a deferred figure to come
   back to, so an authority gap always takes priority over a costing gap,
   including when both are missing at once.
2. If a named authority exists, **is the cost a specific, costed line item,
   or does it defer spend pending a specific missing figure?**
   - Fully costed → the Space Committee accepts the plan as submitted, no
     delay.
   - Defers spend pending a named, specific figure → a two-week delay while
     that figure is confirmed, and any other commitment already made in the
     record (an accessibility guarantee, for instance) still holds
     regardless of the delay.
3. **Independently of 1–2, for each role in turn: does the record satisfy
   that role's stated minimum acceptable outcome from their role card?** A
   "no" flags the record as contested for that role going into the reveal,
   regardless of whether they spoke up in the room, and regardless of which
   outcome 1–2 produced — a record can be accepted or delayed by rules 1–2
   *and* still be contested under rule 3 for one or more roles. A record
   the text genuinely doesn't confirm either way for a given role is left
   unresolved rather than guessed at, exactly as an evidence-limited
   autopsy would treat it — see the walkthrough below for a case where this
   happens.

Applied to the record above, rule by rule:

- **Rule 1 — named authority?** Yes: "Executive Sponsor signs off the
  proposal going forward." Proceed to rule 2.
- **Rule 2 — cost complete or deferred?** Deferred: the fit-out figure is
  an explicitly named, specific gap ("Unverified assumption: that the
  accessibility fit-out cost... will not exceed the software upgrade
  saving") with an owner and a one-week deadline. → **Two-week delay**,
  accessibility commitment holds.
- **Rule 3 — each role's minimum, checked against the record:**
  - *Delivery Lead* ("a decision that does not commit to a go-live date
    before the software question is answered") — met: the software
    question was answered in the negotiation log before the meeting, and
    the record's phase-in follows from that answer, not ahead of it.
  - *Staff Representative* ("named, reserved desks for anyone with a
    documented accessibility need") — met: the record names reserved
    desks "guaranteed from day one."
  - *Finance and Risk Lead* ("a named, costed proposal... rather than an
    in-principle agreement to spend later") — **not met**: the record's
    own Dissent field already says so directly — conditional support,
    "not approving final spend until the fit-out figure is confirmed in
    writing." → **contested flag** for this role, on top of the rule-2
    delay.
  - *Executive Sponsor* ("any plan that raises reported utilisation above
    50% within two terms") — **undetermined from the record**: the record
    commits to a two-term phase-in but never states a resulting
    utilisation figure, so whether this specific minimum is met cannot be
    read off the text either way. That is a limit of what the record says,
    not a finding that it's unmet — the honest call here is "cannot be
    determined from this record," not a guess in either direction.

Final result: **two-week delay** (rule 2), **contested for the Finance and
Risk Lead** (rule 3), and **no determinable rule-3 result for the Executive
Sponsor** — three separate, individually traceable outputs from one record,
not a single "two-week delay" headline that quietly drops the other two.

### 7. An example retrospective and partial rerun

After the reveal, each person writes a short individual autopsy. One
example, from whoever played the Staff Representative:

> What actually shifted my position wasn't the meeting — it was contacting
> the Delivery Lead beforehand and learning the booking *software* was
> already free under the existing licence. By the time we sat down, I was
> arguing from "this is basically free," not "please don't take our
> desks" — and looking back, that shorthand overstated what I actually
> knew. Only the software licence was confirmed free; the accessibility
> fit-out itself was never costed at that point, and "the software part is
> free" is not the same claim as "the whole thing is free," even though
> that's the version I let the room hear. It's still what changed how much
> the Executive Sponsor was willing to concede, which is worth being honest
> about: it worked partly because it sounded more settled than the evidence
> actually supported. If I hadn't made that contact at all, I think the
> meeting would have produced a straight yes/no vote instead of a combined
> proposal — but the contact earning that outcome doesn't excuse
> overstating what it proved.

The partial rerun makes this same claim testable by labelling exactly what
it changes and what it assumes, rather than leaving "I think" as the final
word:

> **Changed variable:** the Staff Representative's two self-initiated
> contacts go to the Finance and Risk Lead and the Executive Sponsor
> instead of the Delivery Lead.
>
> **Held constant:** every other role's private brief, the meeting length,
> and the order in which items were raised in the room.
>
> **Resulting difference:** without contacting the Delivery Lead, the Staff
> Representative never learns that the booking software is already free
> under the existing licence, and walks into the meeting able to argue only
> "this costs something" — rather than the narrower, accurate claim that
> contact actually supports: "the software side of this costs nothing; the
> fit-out is still an open figure." (The Staff Representative's real
> account above overstated even that narrower claim as "basically free" —
> this rerun states the accurate version instead of repeating the
> overstatement.)
>
> **Unverified assumption:** that the Finance and Risk Lead or Executive
> Sponsor would not have independently surfaced the same licence fact if
> asked directly. Nothing in the record confirms or rules this out — the
> rerun's conclusion holds only as strongly as this assumption does.

This is the format the partial rerun expects: one named change, everything
else pinned down explicitly, the concrete difference that follows from it,
and the one assumption still doing unverified work — not a general
impression of what "probably" would have happened.

## What you submit

See [The Alignment Lab](/assessments/alignment-lab/) for the full marking
breakdown. In short: an initial judgment, a strategy memo, a negotiation log,
one shared decision record, an individual decision autopsy, and a partial
rerun of one key juncture — each modelled above using the public example, and
each available as a starting-point format on the [templates](/templates/)

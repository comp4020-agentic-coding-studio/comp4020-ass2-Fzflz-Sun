# The Alignment Lab

The Alignment Lab is the course's central exercise: a small group is given a
real-shaped decision to make together, each person holding one of four roles
and a private brief the others cannot see in full. It is marked as
[The Alignment Lab](/assessments/alignment-lab/) assessment, worth 45% of the
course.

The scenario used for the graded Lab is confidential and distributed by the
teaching team before your group's session — publishing it here would let
later groups prepare against roles they haven't been assigned yet, which
defeats the exercise. This page instead runs you through the process using a
small, fully public worked example, so you can see exactly what a role card
and a decision record look like before you are handed the real ones.

## The four roles

Every Lab scenario is built around the same four functional archetypes, even
though the fictional person and organisation change each time:

- **Executive Sponsor** — owns the outcome the organisation wants, and
  answers for it upward.
- **Delivery Lead** — has to make whatever gets decided actually work.
- **Finance and Risk Lead** — controls whether the decision can be funded,
  and what it might cost if it goes wrong.
- **Staff Representative** — speaks for the people the decision is done to,
  not just the people making it.

Each role's private brief carries the same six things: a public
responsibility everyone can see, one piece of evidence only that role holds,
one constraint they cannot trade away, one form of leverage or dependency
over the others, the minimum outcome they can live with, and the specific
condition under which they would change their position.

## Ground rules, before anyone is assigned a role

These apply to every run of the Lab, and are agreed before role cards are
distributed — not introduced partway through, and not something you discover
only in Week 11's material on the ethics of alignment.

- **No real disputes.** Every scenario is fictional. You will never be asked
  to re-litigate an actual disagreement from your workplace, household or
  student group.
- **Opt-out without penalty.** If a role or scenario would put you in a
  position you are not comfortable with, you can request a swap, or the
  alternative pathway below, by messaging your facilitator any time before
  role cards are distributed — no explanation required. The same pathway is
  also where a student who missed role assignment and the contact window
  entirely is routed — see "Make-up and async participation" below for why
  that case cannot use the ordinary async make-up.
  - **The alternative / structured-async pathway, concretely:** you work
    from this page's public worked example instead of the group's live,
    confidential scenario, but you do not simply read and comment on the
    example materials already on this page. Playing one role through the
    Contact Simulator in Section 2 above (or, as a written equivalent, the
    same role cards' frozen contact transcripts your facilitator can
    provide), you choose whom to contact within the same self-initiated
    contact limit everyone else has, and at every contact log its purpose,
    the new information it surfaced, any commitment made, and whether your
    judgment changed — the same four things a live contact log records.
    That produces your own structured negotiation log, not a summary of the
    page's example one in Section 4. From your own negotiation log and the
    fully revealed role cards, you then independently produce your own
    decision record, in the same format as Section 5's, and write your own
    individual autopsy and partial rerun of it. Every artefact you submit is
    one you produced, not an analysis of one the page already gives you a
    finished answer for.
  - It is marked against the same five criteria as the group-based
    submission (see [the assessment](/assessments/alignment-lab/)), with
    one adjustment. Four of the five — initial judgment and strategy memo
    (20%), pre-meeting negotiation (20%), individual decision autopsy
    (35%), partial rerun (10%) — already mark individual work on a record
    each person produces or works through themselves, so nothing about them
    requires a live group. The fifth, formal decision record (15%), is
    normally the one artefact a live group produces **together** and is
    marked once, identically, for everyone named in it — with no group to
    produce one, this pathway marks it instead on the decision record you
    independently produce from your own negotiation log, treated as an
    individual submission standing in for the shared artefact, not on a
    commentary about the page's example record. See the criterion table
    under "What you submit" near the bottom of this page for exactly which
    pathway each criterion draws its evidence from, and why each is held to
    the same evidentiary standard.
- **In-role, not in-character.** You are arguing a position, not performing a
  persona. Personal attacks, real names, and anything outside the brief are
  out of bounds, and the session facilitator can pause the room if the line
  is crossed.
- **Nothing said in-role is evidence of what you personally believe.** The
  decision autopsy afterwards is marked on your analysis, not on the position
  your role required you to argue.
- **The record is the record.** Whatever the group's decision record says is
  what gets marked as the group's outcome, even if you privately disagreed —
  which is precisely the situation the decision autopsy asks you to examine.
- **Absence doesn't erase information.** From the moment role cards are
  distributed, each person keeps a
  [continuity memo](/templates/) alongside their brief — the same five
  fields every time: current position, commitments made or received,
  evidence surfaced, what's still unresolved, and the single most
  important thing for a replacement to know first. It is updated after
  every contact you log in your negotiation log, and again immediately
  before the meeting starts, so it is never more than one contact out of
  date.
  - **How this reaches the group if you're absent:** if the person holding
    a role cannot attend, the group reads that role's continuity memo
    aloud at the start of the meeting, and plays that position from the
    memo as written — not from guesswork about what the absent person
    "probably" would have argued beyond what the memo actually says.
  - **What it produces, and how it's marked:** the memo is submitted
    alongside your negotiation log. It is not marked as a separate
    criterion; it feeds the "Pre-meeting negotiation" criterion (20%) as
    the evidence that negotiation continuity survived your own possible
    absence, and it protects your own "Individual decision autopsy" mark
    (35%) by giving you, the absent person, a documented position of your
    own to autopsy from even though you were not in the room.
- **Make-up and async participation are always available, and split into two
  genuinely different cases depending on what you actually missed.**
  - **(a) You were assigned a role and had the contact window, but missed
    only the live meeting itself.** Your initial judgment, strategy memo and
    negotiation log are already your own genuine work from before and during
    the real contact window — you submit exactly those, on the same clock as
    everyone else, and do not reconstruct or rewrite them from the group's
    materials after the fact; the point of this case is that you were
    present for everything except the meeting, so nothing about your own
    artefacts up to that point needs redoing. What you didn't get to do is
    help the group produce its decision record live, so once your group does
    meet and produce one, you are given everything it had at the reveal, not
    a summary of it: the full reveal pack (every role's brief, not only your
    own), the group's own negotiation log and contact records, the decision
    record itself, and the continuity memo of the role you're standing in
    for. Using those materials, within one week of receiving them, you audit
    the group's actual decision record against the full reveal pack and
    submit your own corrected or reconstructed decision record: where you
    agree with a field, say so and cite what in the reveal pack supports it;
    where a field is incomplete or wrong against the full pack, name the
    specific correction and the evidence for it. This is scored against the
    same ten fields and the same quality standard as a live record — not
    against whether it matches the group's original, since a well-evidenced
    disagreement with the original record is not itself a mark against you.
    From your own negotiation log, the group's decision record and your
    corrected version of it, you then submit the same individual autopsy and
    partial rerun everyone else does.
  - **(b) You missed role assignment or the contact window itself, not only
    the meeting.** This is not folded into case (a) above — there is no
    genuine negotiation log or strategy memo of your own to submit if you
    were never in the window that would have produced them, and pretending
    to reconstruct one after the fact from the group's record would mean
    submitting someone else's evidence as your own. Instead you are routed
    to the **alternative / structured-async pathway** described under
    "Opt-out without penalty" above, which is built from the start to
    produce every artefact independently rather than assuming a real contact
    window happened for you.
  - **Why no one's grade depends on a groupmate's attendance or
    timeliness:** the negotiation log, strategy memo and individual autopsy
    are all marked as individual work regardless of who else was present or
    how promptly they submitted, and the decision record is finalised at
    the live meeting itself, then marked once, identically, for everyone
    named in it — a groupmate's absence or a late personal submission
    changes how *they* produced their own account of the record, not which
    artefacts exist for anyone else, including you, to be marked on.
  - **Deadlines, and what actually governs them:** the relative windows
    above (48–72 hours for contact, one week for the async make-up) describe
    the normal sequencing of a run, but the fixed date and time on [the
    Alignment Lab assessment page](/assessments/alignment-lab/) is the
    actual deadline for every artefact — none of the relative language
    above extends a submission past it. If your group's meeting or reveal
    lands late enough that a relative window would run past that fixed
    date, the fixed date still governs; raise the timing with your
    facilitator rather than assuming the relative window buys extra time.
- **Peer feedback checks contributions, not popularity.** Any peer input into
  marking asks what a specific person actually contributed — a piece of
  evidence surfaced, a commitment logged, a position changed — never a
  general vote on who did well.
- **You exit the role afterwards.** Once the reveal has happened, the group
  explicitly closes the exercise before moving on: what a role did in the
  simulation is discussed as the role's behaviour, not as a judgement of the
  classmate who played it.

## How a run of the Lab works

0. **Rehearsal.** A small, ungraded run of this whole process — using the
   public worked example below — before anyone sits the assessed Lab.
1. **Public materials and initial judgment.** Before any role is assigned,
   everyone reads the same public materials and submits an individual
   initial judgment: what they think should happen, on the record, before
   anyone has a stake in a role.
2. **Role assignment and strategy memo.** Each person receives one
   confidential role brief and, individually, submits a one-page strategy
   memo: their responsibilities, their evidence, what they don't know, who
   they plan to contact, and the ethical boundaries they won't cross.
3. **Pre-meeting contact window** (48–72 hours). By default, each person may
   make at most **two self-initiated contacts** — but can reply to as many
   requests from others as they like. Every contact, sent or received, is
   logged by both parties: its purpose, any new information, any commitments
   made, and whether either person's judgment changed. The simulator below
   shows the shape of this window using the public worked example.
4. **The meeting.** Live or synchronous, one sitting of around 25 minutes, no
   extensions on the clock once it starts.
5. **Decision record.** The group produces one record — the decision or a
   reasoned deferral, who holds final decision authority over it and
   separately who holds spending and implementation authority (and whether
   each has actually been granted, not just implied), evidence versus
   unverified assumption, unresolved dissent, who owns the resulting actions,
   and what would reopen it — using the
   [decision record template](/templates/). Consensus is not required.
6. **Reveal and consequence test.** Every role brief is shared with the whole
   group, then a choice-based consequence test runs against the decision
   record. Its outcomes come from explicit, traceable case rules, not from
   chance or dramatic licence — it is a teaching model of one plausible
   consequence, not a claim that a real decision like it would play out the
   same way.
7. **Individual autopsy and partial rerun.** Individually: a decision
   autopsy reaching one of three conclusions about what actually decided the
   outcome — a specific distortion, no significant distortion, or
   indeterminate on the available record (naming the missing evidence, the
   alternative you could not rule out, and what would change your
   judgment) — then a short rerun of one specific juncture to test whether a
   named process change would have produced a different one.

## Worked example: should the Reading Room move to hot-desking?

This is a small, entirely public scenario used only to demonstrate the
mechanics above — it is not the graded Lab scenario, and none of the
documents below are marked on how convincing they sound, only on the
evidence and reasoning inside them. It walks through every artefact a real
run produces, end to end, with an example filled in at each step.

Facilities wants to convert 40 assigned desks in the Reading Room into
unassigned hot-desks, to fit a larger cohort. Staff were not consulted before
the proposal was drafted. Four people, one for each archetype, have to reach
a decision.

### 1. Public materials and an example initial judgment

Everyone reads the paragraph above — the same materials, before any role is
assigned — and records what they think should happen. Here is one example of
a filled-in initial judgment:

> Converting to hot-desking might be defensible if utilisation really is
> low, but a proposal drafted with no staff input is a bad way to get there
> even if the destination is right. Before I'd support it, I would want to
> see the actual utilisation data behind the proposal, and confirmation that
> anyone with a documented accessibility need has been asked what they
> require. My initial position: support investigating the change, do not
> support committing to a date yet.

This is submitted on the record before role assignment specifically so it
can later be compared with what the same person argued once they were
holding a role — the gap between the two is often the most interesting
thing in the decision autopsy.

### 3. A filled example strategy memo

Individually, each person turns their role card into a one-page memo before
contacting anyone. Here is the Staff Representative's:

> **My responsibilities:** speak for the 12 staff who currently have an
> assigned desk, and specifically flag anything that affects the 3 with
> fitted accessibility equipment.
>
> **My evidence:** three staff have accessibility equipment permanently
> fitted to their current desk that a hot-desk cannot accommodate without
> notice. I don't yet have a cost estimate for reserved-desk hardware, or
> know whether Facilities has already budgeted for it.
>
> **What I don't know:** whether the booking software has any bearing on
> cost at all, whether the fit-out has been costed by anyone yet, and
> whether Finance would treat these as one proposal or two.
>
> **Who I plan to contact:** the Delivery Lead first, since they own
> whatever the room's technical side involves, to ask what it would
> actually take to guarantee reserved desks and whether that raises a cost
> I don't already know about; the Finance and Risk Lead only if that
> conversation raises a number I need confirmed.
>
> **Ethical boundaries I won't cross:** I will not agree to any timeline
> that has not been taken back to affected staff for comment, regardless of
> what it would cost the group to wait.

### 4. An example negotiation log

The log is not capped at two entries — it is the two *self-initiated*
contacts that are capped; replies to requests from others, and any
follow-up on the same contact, are logged too. Purpose, new information,
any commitment made, and whether either side's judgment changed are all
recorded for every entry:

1. **Staff Representative → Delivery Lead** (self-initiated). *Purpose:*
   find out if the software upgrade and accessibility fit-out could be one
   proposal. *New information:* drawing on the licence terms only they had
   read (their stated leverage on the role card), the Delivery Lead confirms
   the booking software already supports named, reserved desks at no extra
   cost — only genuine unassigned hot-desking needs the paid upgrade.
   *Commitment:* Delivery Lead agrees to draft a combined line item.
   *Judgment change:* Staff Representative's minimum acceptable outcome now
   looks achievable without a fight — a material change from the initial
   judgment above.
2. **Finance and Risk Lead → Staff Representative** (self-initiated).
   *Purpose:* Finance asked what the accessibility fit-out would cost, to
   fold it into the same costed proposal as the software upgrade.
   *New information:* Staff Representative did not have a number, but named
   the Facilities staff member who does. *Commitment:* Staff Representative
   will chase that number before the meeting. *Judgment change:* none
   recorded either side.
3. **Staff Representative → Executive Sponsor** (self-initiated, second and
   final self-initiated contact). *Purpose:* check whether a slower
   phase-in would still count toward this year's target. *New information:*
   Executive Sponsor confirms they would accept a slower phase-in if it
   comes with a firm date. *Commitment:* none made either way. *Judgment
   change:* Executive Sponsor's position moves from "any plan, fast" toward
   "a firm date matters more than speed" — a change worth bringing into the
   meeting.

### 5. A formal decision record example

The record the group actually produces does not have to be a clean
agreement — dissent, conditional support and deferral are all legitimate
outcomes, provided the record says so honestly:

> **Decision:** proceed with a combined proposal — booking-software upgrade
> and reserved accessibility desks — as one line item, with the
> accessibility fit-out still awaiting full costing, phased in
> over two terms with reserved desks guaranteed from day one, **on
> condition that the phase-in timeline is taken back to the 12 affected
> staff for comment before it is finalised.** That condition is not the
> group's addition — it is the Staff Representative's stated non-tradeable
> constraint, so the decision is written as conditional on it rather than
> as settled and separately hoping the constraint gets honoured later.
>
> **Who agreed:** Executive Sponsor, Delivery Lead and Finance and Risk
> Lead agree to the proposal as drafted, including the consultation
> condition above. The Staff Representative's position is recorded as
> **final agreement pending consultation**, not agreement to a settled
> plan: she has not yet agreed to anything final, because her own
> non-tradeable constraint makes the plan's status contingent on a
> consultation that has not happened yet.
>
> **Evidence relied on:** the booking-software licence terms (Delivery
> Lead); the accessibility equipment list (Staff Representative); the
> utilisation report (Executive Sponsor).
>
> **Unverified assumption:** that the accessibility fit-out cost, still
> unconfirmed at meeting time, will not exceed the software upgrade saving
> Finance identified; and that returning the timeline to the 12 affected
> staff for comment will not surface an objection serious enough to reopen
> the phase-in itself.
>
> **Dissent:** Finance and Risk Lead supports the plan **conditionally** —
> on record as not approving final spend until the fit-out figure is
> confirmed in writing. The Staff Representative's final-agreement-pending-
> consultation position above is recorded separately from dissent: she is
> not opposing the plan, she is withholding final agreement until her
> non-tradeable constraint is actually met, which this record treats as a
> different thing from disagreeing with the plan's content.
>
> **Action owner:** Delivery Lead drafts the combined proposal; Staff
> Representative confirms the fit-out figure within one week, and
> separately runs the staff-consultation step before the phase-in date is
> fixed — her constraint was that the timeline goes back to staff before it
> holds, not merely before it is announced.
>
> **Final decision authority:** Executive Sponsor signs off the *proposal
> going forward* once the fit-out figure lands **and** the staff
> consultation above has actually happened — not the group by majority,
> and not a completed decision on either point alone. This sign-off is a
> licence to keep drafting and costing the combined line item; it is not,
> by itself, either of the two authorities below.
>
> **Spending authority:** Finance and Risk Lead, under their standing
> authority over spend above the university's threshold — **not yet
> granted**. The Executive Sponsor's final decision authority above does
> not stand in for this; any actual spend still requires the Finance and
> Risk Lead's own sign-off once the fit-out figure is confirmed, which this
> record does not and cannot override.
>
> **Implementation authority:** Delivery Lead, for the software
> configuration, and Facilities, for the physical fit-out — **not yet
> granted**. Purchasing or installing the accessibility equipment,
> physically reassigning or relabelling any of the 40 desks, and telling
> the 12 affected staff a fixed go-live date are all implementation actions
> that depend on spending authority above being granted first; none of them
> may happen before then.
>
> **What would reopen this record:** a fit-out figure that exceeds the
> software-upgrade saving, Facilities confirming the "no extra cost"
> licence claim was wrong, or the staff consultation surfacing an objection
> to the phase-in that the group has not addressed.

### 6. Consequences, from stated rules

The consequence test is not a dramatised outcome — it applies explicit,
ordered rules to whatever the decision record actually says, so the same
record always produces the same consequence. As of this page, those rules
are not only argued in prose: they are a small, typed, pure function —
`evaluateCostAndAuthority` and `evaluateRoleRule`, defined in
`src/data/consequence-rules.ts` — and the worked result below is that
function's actual output on the record above, not a hand-written paraphrase
of what it should say.

Be precise about what that function does and does not do. It does not read
the prose record above and parse it — no software here extracts "the fit-out
cost is unconfirmed" from a paragraph of English. A human reads the record
and codes its relevant facts into the typed inputs the function actually
takes, `hotDeskingCostAuthorityInput` and `hotDeskingRoleRuleInputs` in
`src/data/consequence-rules.ts` (for instance, `costFullyKnown: false` and
`missingFigure: { name: "the accessibility fit-out cost", owner: "Staff
Representative", deadline: "within one week" }`, coded from the record's own
"Unverified assumption" and "Action owner" fields above). That coding step is
a human judgement call, not a mechanical extraction, which is exactly why the
walkthrough below cites the specific record text behind each field it uses —
so the coding is auditable, not asserted. What the function then guarantees
is only that its rules are applied to that coded input consistently and
deterministically, every time, rather than being re-argued from scratch in
prose on each page that needs the result.

The rules turn on three kinds of authority a record can name, which are not
the same thing and a record naming one does not establish the others:

- **Final decision authority** — who signs off on the decision going
  forward.
- **Spending authority** — who can actually authorise the money moving.
  Holding final decision authority over a record does **not**, on its own,
  mean you hold spending authority over what it costs.
- **Implementation authority** — who can act on the decision in the world
  (buy the equipment, move the desks, announce the date). Nothing
  implementation-shaped may proceed until spending authority has actually
  been obtained, however clearly the decision itself has been signed off.

The rules are checked in this order, because a record can honestly satisfy
more than one trigger at once — "no named final decision authority" and
"defers spend pending a missing figure" are independent facts about a
record, not opposite ends of one switch, so a record missing both can trip
both at the same time unless something decides which one governs:

1. **Is there a named final decision authority?** If no → the Space
   Committee sends the record back for one revision before it will
   consider anything else about it. This is checked first: without someone
   named to hold the record's decisions, there is no one for a deferred
   figure to come back to, so an authority gap always takes priority over a
   costing gap, including when both are missing at once.
2. **Does the record treat that final decision authority's sign-off as
   spending authority?** If yes → sent back for revision regardless of how
   complete the costing is. This is the mistake the worked example's own
   record is careful to avoid: naming a final decision authority is not the
   same fact as that person, or anyone else, having spending authority —
   a record that quietly conflates the two has made an authority error, not
   a costing one, and rule 3 below never gets to evaluate a record that
   makes it.
3. If a final decision authority is named and not mistaken for spending
   authority, **is the cost fully costed and validly authorised?** This
   branch is exhaustive — every remaining record lands in exactly one of
   three buckets, and the test for which one is not just "does the record
   name a missing figure" but "does the record actually withhold spend
   until that figure lands, and has spending authority actually been
   obtained":
   - **Fully costed and validly authorised** → the figure is complete
     *and* the spend has been approved by someone who actually holds
     spending authority → the Space Committee accepts the plan as
     submitted, no delay.
   - **Specific cost explicitly deferred** — the record says exactly which
     number is missing, who owns getting it, and the deadline, **and
     explicitly prohibits any dependent spend until that figure is
     confirmed and validly approved** → a one-week delay while that figure
     is confirmed, and any other commitment already made in the record (an
     accessibility guarantee, for instance) still holds regardless of the
     delay.
   - **Uncosted, prematurely authorised, or authorised by the wrong
     authority** — this branch covers four distinct ways a record can fail
     to earn acceptance or a genuine deferral: a vague "cost to be
     confirmed" that names no figure, owner or deadline; a record that
     *does* name the missing figure and its owner but still authorises the
     dependent spend as if the figure were already confirmed; a record
     where the cost is fully known but no one who actually holds spending
     authority has approved it; and (caught earlier, at rule 2) a record
     where final decision authority is wrongly treated as spending
     authority. Naming the gap does not move a record out of this branch;
     only actually withholding spend until the gap is closed, *and*
     getting the spend approved by someone with real spending authority,
     does. This is a harder stop than the middle branch: a genuine
     deferral has a concrete number to wait for and nothing spend-related
     moves until it lands, so the rest of the record can proceed around
     it; a record that authorises spend regardless — or that was never
     going to be approved by anyone with the authority to approve it — has
     nothing left to wait for by the time the Committee sees it, so
     nothing in it is treated as settled until it is re-drafted around an
     actual, honoured deferral and a valid approval.
4. **Independently of 1–3, for each role in turn: does the record satisfy
   *both* that role's stated minimum acceptable outcome *and* their
   non-tradeable constraint from their role card, checked together rather
   than as alternatives?** Meeting the minimum outcome does not excuse
   silently overriding the constraint, and honouring the constraint does
   not excuse falling short of the minimum outcome — either gap on its own
   flags the record as contested for that role going into the reveal,
   regardless of whether they spoke up in the room, and regardless of which
   outcome 1–3 produced. A record can be accepted or delayed by rules 1–3
   *and* still be contested under rule 4 for one or more roles. A record
   the text genuinely doesn't confirm either way for a given role — on the
   outcome, the constraint, or both — is left unresolved rather than
   guessed at, exactly as an evidence-limited autopsy would treat it — see
   the walkthrough below for a case where this happens.

Applied to the record above, rule by rule — each bullet names what the
function actually returned, computed live from
`hotDeskingCostAuthorityInput` and `hotDeskingRoleRuleInputs`:

- **Rule 1 — named final decision authority?** Yes: "Executive Sponsor
  signs off the proposal going forward." Proceed to rule 2.
- **Rule 2 — final authority mistaken for spending authority?** No: the
  record is explicit that the sponsor's sign-off "authorises drafting and
  costing the combined line item; it does not itself authorise spending."
  Proceed to rule 3.
- **Rule 3 — which of the three cost branches?** The function's computed
  result: consequence **{hotDeskingCostAuthorityOutcome.consequence}**
  ({hotDeskingCostAuthorityOutcome.reason}). This is the middle branch, not
  the third one: the fit-out figure is an explicitly named, specific gap
  ("Unverified assumption: that the accessibility fit-out cost... will not
  exceed the software upgrade saving") with an owner
  ({hotDeskingCostAuthorityInput.missingFigure?.owner}) and a
  ({hotDeskingCostAuthorityInput.missingFigure?.deadline}) deadline, so
  there is a concrete number and date to wait for rather than an
  open-ended "cost to be confirmed." Just as important, the record
  actually withholds spend on it, with any real spend requiring the
  Finance and Risk Lead's own sign-off first — naming the gap alone would
  not be enough to land here; it is the combination of naming it *and* not
  spending through it that keeps this record out of the third branch. →
  **One-week delay**, accessibility commitment holds.
- **Rule 4 — each role's minimum outcome *and* constraint, checked
  together:**
  - *Delivery Lead* — minimum outcome ("a decision that does not commit to
    a go-live date before the software question is answered") met: the
    software question was answered in the negotiation log before the
    meeting, and the record's phase-in follows from that answer, not
    ahead of it. Constraint ("unassigned hot-desking cannot proceed until
    the paid software upgrade it requires has an approved budget and valid
    spending authority behind it") **honoured, not bypassed**: the record's
    own Implementation authority field states plainly that the Delivery

  - *Finance and Risk Lead* — minimum outcome ("a named, costed

### 7. An example retrospective and partial rerun

After the reveal, each person writes a short individual autopsy. One
example, from whoever played the Staff Representative:

> What actually shifted my position wasn't the meeting — it was contacting
> the Delivery Lead beforehand and learning the booking *software* was
> already free under the existing licence. By the time we sat down, I was
> arguing from "this is basically free," not "please don't take our
> desks" — and looking back, that shorthand overstated what I actually
> knew. Only the software licence was confirmed free; the accessibility
> fit-out itself was never costed at that point, and "the software part is
> free" is not the same claim as "the whole thing is free," even though
> that's the version I let the room hear. It's still what changed how much
> the Executive Sponsor was willing to concede, which is worth being honest
> about: it worked partly because it sounded more settled than the evidence
> actually supported. If I hadn't made that contact at all, I think the
> meeting would have produced a straight yes/no vote instead of a combined
> proposal — but the contact earning that outcome doesn't excuse
> overstating what it proved.

The partial rerun makes this same claim testable by labelling exactly what
it changes and what it assumes, rather than leaving "I think" as the final
word:

> **Changed variable:** the Staff Representative's two self-initiated
> contacts go to the Finance and Risk Lead and the Executive Sponsor
> instead of the Delivery Lead.
>
> **Held constant:** every other role's private brief, the meeting length,
> and the order in which items were raised in the room.
>
> **Resulting difference:** without contacting the Delivery Lead, the Staff
> Representative never learns that the booking software is already free
> under the existing licence, and walks into the meeting able to argue only
> "this costs something" — rather than the narrower, accurate claim that
> contact actually supports: "the software side of this costs nothing; the
> fit-out is still an open figure." (The Staff Representative's real
> account above overstated even that narrower claim as "basically free" —
> this rerun states the accurate version instead of repeating the
> overstatement.)
>
> **Unverified assumption:** that the Finance and Risk Lead or Executive
> Sponsor would not have independently surfaced the same licence fact if
> asked directly. Nothing in the record confirms or rules this out — the
> rerun's conclusion holds only as strongly as this assumption does.

This is the format the partial rerun expects: one named change, everything
else pinned down explicitly, the concrete difference that follows from it,
and the one assumption still doing unverified work — not a general
impression of what "probably" would have happened.

## What you submit

See [The Alignment Lab](/assessments/alignment-lab/) for the full marking
breakdown. In short: an initial judgment, a strategy memo, a negotiation log,
a decision record — produced with your live group, or individually under the
async make-up or alternative pathway, per the breakdown below — an individual
decision autopsy, and a partial rerun of one key juncture — each modelled
above using the public example, and each available as a starting-point format
on the [templates](/templates/) page.

Each criterion's evidence comes from a different place depending on which of
the three pathways you're on — live, async make-up (you had a role and a
contact window, but missed only the live meeting — case (a) above), or the
alternative / structured-async pathway (an opt-out, or case (b) above: you
missed role assignment or the contact window itself). The weights and total
(100% of the Lab's own mark) are the same across all three; only where the
evidence for each one comes from, and what counts as meeting the standard,
changes:

| Criterion (weight, level) | Live | Async make-up | Alternative / structured-async |
|---|---|---|---|
| Initial judgment & strategy memo (20%, individual) | Written before role cards / before contact opens | Same artefact, written on the same clock as everyone else, before you knew you'd miss the meeting — not rewritten afterwards | Written from this page's public worked example, before you touch the Contact Simulator or any frozen transcript |
| Pre-meeting negotiation (20%, individual) | Your own logged contacts during the live window | Your own logged contacts from that same real window — you were present for it — not reconstructed afterwards from the group's shared materials | Your own contacts logged while playing the worked example through the Contact Simulator (or its frozen transcripts), under the same self-initiated-contact limit |
| Formal decision record (15%, **group** in the live case; an individually-produced equivalent otherwise) | One record your live group produces together; marked once for everyone named in it | No group record to co-produce live — you audit the group's actual record against the full reveal pack and submit your own corrected or reconstructed record, scored against the same ten fields and standard as a live one | No group exists — you independently produce your own decision record from your own negotiation log and the worked example's fully revealed role cards |
| Individual decision autopsy (35%, individual) | Individually, after your group's own reveal | Individually, from the received record, full reveal pack and your own logged contacts | Individually, of the decision record you produced above |
| Partial rerun (10%, individual) | One juncture from your live meeting | Same, reasoned from the written materials you received | One juncture from your own Contact Simulator run or frozen transcript |

### Why each row is equivalent, not merely parallel

Four of the five rows are individual work under every pathway; only the
decision record briefly reads as "shared" in the live case, which is exactly
why it gets its own, longer reasoning below rather than the others' shorter
one.

- **Initial judgment & strategy memo.** What's marked is reasoning produced
  before any negotiated information existed — every pathway enforces that
  same ordering (before role cards / contact for the live and async make-up
  cases, before touching the simulator for the alternative pathway), so the
  task tests the same skill regardless of which materials supplied it.
- **Pre-meeting negotiation.** What's marked is whether each logged contact
  records its purpose, the new information it surfaced, any commitment made,
  and whether judgment changed — not whether the contact happened inside a
  real-time group. The Contact Simulator enforces the same self-initiated
  contact cap and reveals the same shape of fact-plus-constraint a live
  contact would, so a log built by playing it through is evidence of the same
  skill — using a limited number of contact opportunities to gather and act
  on evidence — under a worked rather than live counterpart, not a smaller
  version of the task.
- **Formal decision record.** In every pathway this is marked against the
  same ten fields on the [decision record template](/templates/) (the
  decision, evidence relied on, unverified assumptions, who agreed, who
  dissented, action owners, final decision authority, spending authority,
  implementation authority, and what would reopen the record) to the same
  standard of evidence-use. What differs is only who supplies the underlying
  negotiation the record is built from — a live group, an audited correction
  of one, or a record you produce yourself — never whether you personally sat
  in a room. A defensible, well-evidenced correction to the group's own
  record (async make-up) is treated exactly as favourably as a defensible
  original one (alternative), because the criterion asks whether the record
  meets the standard, not whether it agrees with someone else's draft.
- **Individual decision autopsy.** The autopsy is marked on the reasoning
  applied to a decision record and the role briefs behind it, not on whether
  the record was produced live in front of the writer. A record read
  carefully in full, alongside the complete reveal pack, offers the same
  evidentiary base as one watched being produced — the task was never
  "recall what you personally witnessed," it was always "reason from a
  documented record together with the evidence behind it," which every
  pathway supplies.
- **Partial rerun.** A rerun only ever needs one concrete juncture, one named
  process change, and a defended prediction of the concrete difference that
  follows. A documented juncture — from written make-up materials or a
  logged Contact Simulator run — supplies exactly those same three things a
  live juncture would; nothing about the task requires having lived the
  juncture rather than read it precisely.
